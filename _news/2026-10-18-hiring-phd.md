Would you like to do a Ph.D. at the interface of micro/nanotechnology, biomaterials and biomedical
engineering, on a clinically relevant challenge? Join DYNAMO within the interdisciplinary IDUN research
section at DTU Health Tech, and develop near-infrared (NIR) light-activated micromotors for hidradenitis
suppurativa (HS), a chronic inflammatory skin disease.

In this project you will design biodegradable Janus micromotors and build three-dimensional hydrogel
models that reproduce the tunnels formed in HS. You will study how the micromotors' properties and the
confined geometry affect their active transport, and compare it with particles that don't propel
themselves.

### What you will do

- Design and fabricate NIR-responsive Janus micromotors with controlled size and surface properties
- Characterise their morphology, optical and photothermal properties, stability and biodegradability
- Evaluate NIR-activated propulsion in controlled and skin-relevant environments
- Develop 3D hydrogel models mimicking the confined architecture of HS tunnels, with channels of different
  dimensions, branches and bifurcations
- Quantify micromotor penetration, velocity and navigation through these channels, and compare active
  transport with passive diffusion and non-propelled particles
- Share the results through publications, international conferences and collaborations

### Who we are looking for

- A two-year master's degree (120 ECTS) or equivalent, in nanotechnology, chemistry, materials science,
  biomedical engineering, pharmaceutical sciences, biotechnology or a closely related field. You may apply
  before you have your master's degree, but cannot start before you have received it
- A strong interest in micro- and nanotechnology and hands-on experimental research
- Experience with particle fabrication, functional materials, surface modification, microscopy,
  microfabrication or biomaterials is highly desirable. Experience with light-responsive or photothermal
  materials, micromotors, hydrogel fabrication, microfluidics or quantitative particle tracking is an
  advantage
- Curiosity, motivation, and enjoyment of experimental, interdisciplinary work, both independently and in
  a team
