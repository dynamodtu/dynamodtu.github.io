Are you passionate about microrobotics, nanotechnology and innovative biomedical technologies? We are
looking for a motivated postdoc to develop near-infrared (NIR) light-driven micromotors that break through
bacterial biofilms, toward new treatments for hidradenitis suppurativa (HS), a chronic inflammatory skin
disease.

You will join DYNAMO within the interdisciplinary IDUN research section at DTU Health Tech, and take a
leading role in the project, at the interface of micro/nanotechnology, drug delivery, biomaterials and
disease modelling. The aim is a new active drug-delivery strategy for HS, with broader insights into
treating biofilm-associated infections.

### What you will do

- Design and fabricate NIR light-activated micromotors with efficient propulsion
- Characterise their motion, stability, penetration and drug release in biofilm-relevant environments
- Evaluate biofilm penetration, disruption and local drug delivery in vitro
- Contribute to in vivo validation in relevant HS or biofilm-associated disease models
- Evaluate antibacterial activity, drug penetration and overall therapeutic efficacy
- Co-supervise M.Sc. and Ph.D. students, and share the results through publications and conferences

### Who we are looking for

- A Ph.D. (or equivalent) in pharmaceutical sciences, nanotechnology, chemistry, materials science,
  biomedical engineering or a closely related field
- Strong hands-on experience in the design, fabrication and characterisation of micro- and/or nanomotors
- A background in functional materials, surface engineering or drug delivery is highly desirable.
  Experience with light-responsive or NIR-activated systems, biofilms, bacterial models, drug penetration
  or antimicrobial studies, and ex vivo or in vivo models, is an advantage
- An independent, highly motivated researcher with a strong publication record

### The position

- **Duration:** 2 years, full time
- **Start:** 1 February 2027, or by agreement
- **Salary and terms:** based on the collective agreement with the Danish Confederation of Professional
  Associations
